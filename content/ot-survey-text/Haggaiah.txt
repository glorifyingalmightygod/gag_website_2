# HAGGAI Survey

# Introduction

The **Book of Haggai** is the tenth book among the Twelve Minor Prophets in the traditional Christian ordering of the Old Testament. It contains **two chapters** and is one of the shortest books of the Old Testament.

Haggai ministered after the Babylonian exile, during the period when the Jewish people had returned to Jerusalem and Judah under the rule of the Persian Empire.

The central issue of the book was simple but profound:

**The people had returned to the land, but they had neglected the rebuilding of the temple.**

The people were focused on their own houses and personal interests while God's house remained unfinished.

Haggai therefore called them to:

> **"Consider your ways." — Haggai 1:5**

The prophet challenged the people to put God at the center of their priorities.

The book then moves from:

**Neglected Temple → Conviction → Obedience → Encouragement → God's Presence → Future Glory**

Haggai is especially important for understanding the spiritual significance of the **Second Temple** and the restoration period following Judah's exile.

## Main Focus of Haggai

The central message can be summarized as:

**Put God first, obey His word, rebuild what has been neglected, and trust His promises concerning the future.**

## Relevance Today

Haggai speaks powerfully about:

* priorities
* spiritual procrastination
* obedience
* stewardship
* putting God first
* discouragement
* serving God together
* God's presence
* holiness
* perseverance
* future hope

The book challenges believers to examine whether God's work has been pushed aside while personal concerns receive first priority.

# Name of the Book

## Hebrew Name

The Hebrew name is:

**חַגַּי — Ḥaggay / Haggai**

The name is related to the Hebrew word **חַג (ḥag)**, meaning:

**"festival" or "feast."**

Haggai may therefore carry the sense of:

**"festive"** or **"my feast."**

The exact significance of the prophet's name is uncertain.

## Why Is the Book Called Haggai?

The book is named after the prophet who delivered its messages.

Haggai 1:1 says:

> "The word of the LORD came by the hand of Haggai the prophet..."

Unlike Isaiah or Jeremiah, we know very little about Haggai personally.

His prophetic ministry is primarily known through the dated messages preserved in the book.

---

# Author of the Book

## Traditional Author

The traditional author is **Haggai the prophet**.

The book repeatedly identifies Haggai as the recipient and messenger of God's word.

See:

* Haggai 1:1
* Haggai 1:3
* Haggai 2:1
* Haggai 2:10
* Haggai 2:20

## Internal Evidence

Haggai provides unusually precise dates for his prophetic messages.

His ministry occurred during the reign of:

**Darius I of Persia.**

The book identifies Haggai as:

> "Haggai the prophet."

His primary responsibility was to communicate God's word concerning the temple and the people's spiritual priorities.

## External Evidence

Haggai belongs to the collection known as the **Twelve Minor Prophets**.

His historical setting is also supported by the broader biblical account of the post-exilic community in:

* Ezra
* Zechariah

Ezra 5:1 specifically names **Haggai and Zechariah** as prophets who ministered to the Jews in Jerusalem.

## Scholarly Considerations

There is relatively little debate concerning the historical identity of Haggai.

The primary scholarly discussions concern:

* the precise interpretation of some passages
* the relationship between Haggai's temple prophecy and the later Second Temple
* the meaning of Haggai 2:6–9
* the identity and significance of Zerubbabel
* the extent to which some promises have an eschatological dimension

The book itself presents Haggai's messages as genuine prophetic words delivered during the Persian period.

# Audience / To Whom Was It Written

Haggai primarily addressed the **post-exilic Jewish community in Judah and Jerusalem**.

Specific individuals addressed include:

* **Zerubbabel**, governor of Judah
* **Joshua/Jeshua**, the high priest
* the returned Jewish community

Haggai 1:2 refers to:

> "the remnant of the people"

The people had returned from Babylon but faced:

* economic hardship
* discouragement
* opposition
* unfinished rebuilding
* spiritual complacency

Haggai called them to resume the work of rebuilding the temple.

# Historical and Geographical Setting

## 1. The Babylonian Exile

Jerusalem had been destroyed by Babylon in **586 BC**.

The temple built under Solomon was destroyed.

Many Jews were taken into exile.

See:

* 2 Kings 25
* 2 Chronicles 36:15–21
* Jeremiah 52

## 2. Persia Conquers Babylon

In **539 BC**, Cyrus of Persia conquered Babylon.

The Persian Empire adopted policies that allowed displaced peoples to return to their homelands.

Jewish exiles returned to Judah.

## 3. The Return to Jerusalem

The return is described in:

* Ezra 1–2
* Nehemiah 1–3

The returned community began rebuilding Jerusalem and the temple.

## 4. The Temple Project Stalls

The temple rebuilding began but later stalled.

Ezra 4 describes opposition to the rebuilding efforts.

By Haggai's time, the people had become preoccupied with their own houses.

Haggai 1:4 asks:

> "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?"

## 5. The Persian Empire

Haggai ministered under **Darius I**, one of the great Persian kings.

The Persian government controlled Judah.

Judah was no longer an independent kingdom ruled by a Davidic king.

This makes Haggai's references to **Zerubbabel** particularly significant.

# Purpose of the Book

## 1. To Call the People to Rebuild the Temple

The immediate practical purpose is to restart the temple project.

## 2. To Correct Wrong Priorities

The people had invested heavily in their own homes while God's temple remained unfinished.

## 3. To Call the People to Obedience

God repeatedly calls them to act.

## 4. To Explain Their Difficult Circumstances

Haggai connects their economic difficulties with their misplaced priorities.

## 5. To Encourage Discouraged Builders

The people needed courage to continue the work.

## 6. To Assure Them of God's Presence

One of the book's great promises is:

> "I am with you, declares the LORD." — Haggai 1:13

## 7. To Give Hope for Future Glory

Haggai 2 points beyond the immediate rebuilding project toward God's future purposes.

# Key Verses

## Haggai 1:5

> **"Now, therefore, thus says the LORD of hosts: Consider your ways."**

### Explanation

God calls the people to stop and examine their priorities.

### Significance

Spiritual renewal often begins with honest self-examination.

---

## Haggai 1:8

> **"Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house..."**

### Explanation

God gives the people a practical command.

They are not merely called to feel guilty.

They are called to act.

### Significance

True repentance produces obedient action.

---

## Haggai 1:13

> **"I am with you, declares the LORD."**

### Explanation

After the people respond obediently, God encourages them with His presence.

### Significance

God's presence is greater than the difficulty of the assignment.

---

## Haggai 2:4

> **"Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel... Be strong, O Joshua... Be strong, all you people of the land... and work, for I am with you, declares the LORD of hosts."**

### Explanation

God commands courage and action.

### Significance

The foundation of their strength is not human ability.

It is God's presence.

---

## Haggai 2:6

> **"Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth..."**

### Explanation

God announces a future divine intervention affecting nations and creation.

### Significance

The rebuilding of the temple is connected with God's larger purposes for history.

---

## Haggai 2:9

> **"The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former..."**

### Explanation

God promises a future glory greater than what the people remembered from Solomon's temple.

The precise fulfillment and interpretation of this prophecy have been understood in different ways.

### Significance

The passage points beyond immediate discouragement toward God's future purposes.

---

## Haggai 2:23

> **"I will take you, O Zerubbabel my servant... and make you like a signet ring, for I have chosen you, declares the LORD of hosts."**

### Explanation

Zerubbabel receives a remarkable promise of divine choice.

### Significance

This language recalls Davidic royal imagery and contributes to the Old Testament's continuing expectation concerning God's covenant purposes.

# Chapter-by-Chapter Summary

## Chapter 1 – Consider Your Ways

Haggai's first message comes in the second year of Darius.

God confronts the people's priorities.

They say:

> "The time has not yet come to rebuild the house of the LORD." — Haggai 1:2

But they are living comfortably in their own houses while the temple remains in ruins.

## Their Misplaced Priorities

Haggai asks why they are focusing on themselves while God's house remains neglected.

The people experience:

* much sowing but little harvest
* eating without satisfaction
* drinking without being filled
* earning wages that seem to disappear
* economic frustration

Haggai 1:6 describes this vividly.

## God's Command

God says:

> "Consider your ways."

Then He tells them:

> "Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house."

## The People's Response

Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the remnant obey.

Haggai 1:12 says:

> "They obeyed the voice of the LORD their God."

## God's Encouragement

After their obedience, God says:

> "I am with you."

### Spiritual Lessons

* Examine your priorities.
* Obedience should follow conviction.
* Spiritual procrastination can become a lifestyle.
* God's work should not continually be postponed.
* God's presence accompanies faithful obedience.

---

## Chapter 2 – Be Strong and Do Not Fear

Chapter 2 contains three separate messages.

### First Message: October 520 BC

The people become discouraged because the new temple does not appear as glorious as Solomon's temple.

Some older people remembered the former temple.

The new structure seemed inferior.

God therefore tells them:

> "Be strong... and work."

The reason?

> "For I am with you."

## Future Glory

God promises:

> "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."

He also promises:

> "And in this place I will give peace."

This becomes one of the book's most discussed prophetic passages.

---

## Second Message: Holiness and Blessing

God asks the priests questions concerning ritual holiness.

The principle is that holiness is not automatically transferred simply by physical contact.

But impurity can defile.

The people are then told to consider their past circumstances.

The emphasis is:

**obedience and spiritual condition matter.**

God promises blessing from the time they obey.

---

## Third Message: Zerubbabel

The final message is addressed to Zerubbabel.

God says:

> "I am about to shake the heavens and the earth."

God will overthrow kingdoms and establish His purposes.

Then God says:

> "I will take you, O Zerubbabel my servant..."

and compares him to a **signet ring**.

### Spiritual Lessons

* Do not despise small beginnings.
* God's presence matters more than outward appearance.
* Past glory should not prevent present obedience.
* Holiness matters.
* God can use ordinary work for significant purposes.
* God's kingdom purposes extend beyond immediate circumstances.
* God's promises provide courage for faithful service.

# Life Lessons from the Book

## 1. Examine Your Priorities

Haggai's central question remains relevant:

**What receives our best attention, time, resources, and energy?**

## 2. Do Not Put God Permanently on Hold

The people kept postponing the temple project.

Spiritual procrastination can become spiritual neglect.

## 3. Obedience Requires Action

God did not merely ask the people to agree with Him.

He told them to:

**"Go... bring wood... build."**

## 4. God's Work Requires Cooperation

The rebuilding involved:

* leaders
* priests
* ordinary people

God's work was a community responsibility.

## 5. God's Presence Is More Important Than Human Strength

God repeatedly encourages them with:

> "I am with you."

## 6. Do Not Let Comparison Discourage You

The new temple seemed inferior to Solomon's temple.

Yet God told them to continue.

## 7. Do Not Despise Small Beginnings

A project may look unimpressive at first but still be part of God's purpose.

## 8. Holiness Matters

Haggai 2 teaches that spiritual impurity cannot simply be ignored.

## 9. Obedience Can Change the Direction of a Community

The people heard God's word, responded, and began rebuilding.

## 10. God Can Use Ordinary Work

Building a physical structure became an act of worship because it was done in obedience to God.

## 11. God's Promises Give Strength

The people were called to work because God had promised His presence.

## 12. God Controls History

The shaking of nations in Haggai 2 reminds readers that earthly political powers do not have ultimate control.

# Foreshadows / Types of Jesus Christ

Haggai contains important Christological themes, although not every passage should be treated as a direct prediction of Christ.

## 1. Zerubbabel and the Davidic Line

Zerubbabel was a descendant of David.

He appears in:

* Matthew 1:12–13
* 1 Chronicles 3:17–19
* Ezra 3:2

Haggai calls him:

> "my servant"

and compares him to a **signet ring**.

This recalls the significance of royal authority and Davidic covenant imagery.

See also:

* 2 Samuel 7:12–16
* Jeremiah 22:24–30
* Haggai 2:20–23

Haggai does not say that Zerubbabel himself is the Messiah. Rather, his position within the Davidic line contributes to the Old Testament's continuing messianic expectation.

## 2. The Greater Glory of the Temple

Haggai 2:9 says:

> "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."

Interpretations vary.

Some understand the immediate reference as God's future blessing and glory associated with the Second Temple.

Christian interpretation has also connected the passage with Christ's presence in the temple and the greater salvation revealed through Him.

See:

* Luke 2:22–38
* John 2:13–22
* Malachi 3:1
* Hebrews 9:11–14

The connection should be understood as a theological and canonical reading rather than assuming that every detail of the verse is a direct prediction of Jesus.

## 3. God's Presence

Haggai repeatedly emphasizes:

> "I am with you."

The New Testament presents Jesus as **Immanuel — God with us**.

See:

* Matthew 1:23
* John 1:14
* Matthew 28:20

## 4. The Shaking of the Nations

Haggai 2:6–7 speaks of God shaking the heavens and the earth and the nations.

The New Testament develops the theme of a coming divine kingdom that cannot be shaken.

See:

* Hebrews 12:26–28
* Revelation 11:15

## 5. God's House and Christ

The temple theme develops throughout Scripture.

The New Testament identifies Christ as the true meeting place between God and humanity:

* John 1:14
* John 2:19–21

Believers are also described corporately as God's temple:

* 1 Corinthians 3:16
* Ephesians 2:19–22
* 1 Peter 2:4–5

Thus Haggai's temple theology contributes to the larger biblical story that reaches its fulfillment in Christ and the people of God.

# Additional Important Information

## 1. Haggai Is One of the Most Precisely Dated Prophetic Books

Many prophetic books require historical reconstruction to determine their approximate dates.

Haggai is different.

The prophet gives exact dates for his messages.

All four messages occur within approximately four months in **520 BC**.

This makes Haggai particularly useful for historical study.

---

## 2. Haggai and the Second Temple

The temple being rebuilt was the **Second Temple**.

It was not identical in glory or scale to Solomon's original temple.

The rebuilding project began after the exile and was eventually completed under Persian rule.

Ezra 6:15 places its completion in the sixth year of Darius.

---

## 3. The Second Temple Was Later Expanded

The temple structure associated with the post-exilic period was later substantially expanded and beautified under **Herod the Great**.

This is the temple complex known from the time of Jesus.

Therefore, when studying Haggai's temple, it is important to distinguish:

**Zerubbabel's Second Temple → later Herodian expansion → New Testament temple setting.**

---

## 4. "Consider Your Ways"

The repeated challenge to "consider" or carefully reflect on one's ways is central to the book.

Haggai does not merely condemn the people.

He asks them to examine the relationship between:

**their priorities → their behavior → their spiritual condition → their circumstances.**

This makes the book especially useful for discipleship and spiritual self-examination.

---

## 5. Was Their Economic Hardship Directly Caused by Their Sin?

Haggai clearly connects the people's difficult agricultural circumstances with their failure to prioritize the temple.

See:

* Haggai 1:5–11
* Haggai 2:15–19

However, this should not be generalized into the claim that **every individual's financial hardship is caused by personal sin**.

Other biblical books explicitly show that righteous people can suffer.

See:

* Job
* Psalm 73
* John 9:1–3

Haggai's message concerns the specific covenant community and historical situation being addressed by the prophet.

---

## 6. Haggai and Zerubbabel

Zerubbabel was an important post-exilic leader.

He was:

* governor of Judah
* descendant of David
* involved in rebuilding the temple
* contemporary with Joshua the high priest

His presence is important because the Davidic monarchy had disappeared politically, yet the Davidic family continued.

Haggai 2:23 therefore contributes to the biblical theme of God's continuing covenant purposes.

---

## 7. Haggai and Joshua the High Priest

Joshua/Jeshua was the high priest.

He worked alongside Zerubbabel.

This gives the restoration community two important leadership structures:

**Zerubbabel → civil/governor leadership**

**Joshua → priestly/religious leadership**

Together they represent the restored community's political and religious leadership.

---

## 8. Haggai and Zechariah

Haggai and Zechariah should be studied together.

### Haggai

Emphasizes:

* rebuilding
* priorities
* obedience
* encouragement
* temple
* God's presence

### Zechariah

Emphasizes:

* visions
* spiritual restoration
* Joshua
* Zerubbabel
* God's future kingdom
* messianic hope

Ezra presents them as contemporaries.

---

## 9. "I Am With You"

This phrase is one of the most important theological ideas in Haggai.

The people were facing a difficult task.

God's encouragement was not primarily:

**"The work will be easy."**

It was:

**"I am with you."**

This principle appears throughout Scripture.

Compare:

* Exodus 3:12
* Joshua 1:5–9
* Isaiah 41:10
* Matthew 28:20

---

## 10. "The Latter Glory of This House"

Haggai 2:9 has generated substantial interpretive discussion.

Possible approaches include:

### Immediate Historical Interpretation

God promises that the restored temple will receive His glory and become a place of renewed worship.

### Messianic/Christological Interpretation

Christian interpretation often connects the greater glory with Christ's presence and the salvation He brings.

### Eschatological Interpretation

Some understand the passage as pointing toward a future, ultimate manifestation of God's kingdom and glory.

These interpretations are not all mutually exclusive, but the precise fulfillment should be handled carefully.

---

## 11. "The Desire of All Nations"

Some English translations render Haggai 2:7 with language such as:

**"the desire of all nations shall come."**

This phrase has historically been interpreted by some Christians as a direct reference to Christ.

However, the Hebrew wording is grammatically and contextually debated.

Other translations understand the passage more along the lines of:

**"the treasures/wealth of all nations shall come."**

Therefore, it is better not to make the traditional English rendering alone the foundation of a direct messianic prophecy.

The broader passage clearly emphasizes God's future shaking of nations and the glory of His house.

---

## 12. Haggai's Four Messages

The book can be remembered through four commands/themes:

### Message 1

**Consider your ways.**

### Message 2

**Be strong and work.**

### Message 3

**Consider from this day onward.**

### Message 4

**God has chosen.**

This gives the book a strong teaching structure.

---

## 13. Haggai's Main Contrast

The book contrasts:

**Self-centered living**

with

**God-centered obedience.**

The people had built their own houses while God's house remained in ruins.

The problem was therefore deeper than architecture.

It was a problem of **priority**.

---

## 14. Haggai and Christian Stewardship

Haggai provides a useful biblical framework for teaching about stewardship.

Questions for believers include:

* What receives our first attention?
* What receives our best resources?
* What receives our strongest commitment?
* What work for God have we repeatedly postponed?
* Are we building our own "houses" while neglecting God's purposes?

The application should be made carefully. Haggai specifically addressed the temple-building obligation of the post-exilic covenant community.

---

## 15. Haggai and the Church

The New Testament does not command Christians to rebuild the Jerusalem temple.

Instead, temple theology develops in Christ and His people.

See:

* John 2:19–21
* 1 Corinthians 3:16
* 1 Corinthians 6:19
* Ephesians 2:21–22
* 1 Peter 2:5

Therefore, a Christian application of Haggai should focus primarily on:

**God's presence, obedience, worship, priorities, holiness, service, and kingdom purposes**, rather than simply applying the temple construction project literally.

---

## 16. Haggai and Jesus

The Gospel writers place Jesus in the temple during His earthly ministry.

Jesus' teaching about the temple becomes especially significant because John identifies Jesus' body with the temple:

> "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." — John 2:19

John explains that Jesus was speaking about His body.

This represents a major development in biblical temple theology.

---

## 17. Haggai's Historical Significance

Haggai provides an important window into the period between:

**Old Testament monarchy → exile → Persian restoration → Second Temple Judaism**

It helps explain how Jewish religious life was rebuilt after the catastrophe of 586 BC.

---

# Conclusion

The Book of Haggai is short, but its message is powerful.

The returned Jewish community had experienced exile and returned to Jerusalem.

Yet they had become discouraged and distracted.

Their own houses were receiving attention while the temple remained unfinished.

God therefore sent Haggai with a direct challenge:

> **"Consider your ways."**

The people responded.

They obeyed.

They rebuilt.

And God encouraged them:

> **"I am with you."**

The book then moves beyond the immediate construction project.

God promises:

* His presence
* future glory
* blessing
* shaking of nations
* fulfillment of His purposes
* continuing significance for the Davidic line

Haggai teaches that the central issue was never merely **building a building**.

The deeper issue was:

**Who comes first?**

The book calls God's people to examine their priorities, respond to God's word, persevere when the work appears small, and trust God's presence and promises.

### One-Sentence Summary

**Haggai calls God's people to put Him first, obey His word, faithfully rebuild what has been neglected, and trust that His presence and purposes are greater than present discouragement.**

### Key Message

**"Consider your ways." — Haggai 1:5**

### Spiritual Journey of Haggai

**Neglect → Conviction → Obedience → Encouragement → Work → God's Presence → Future Glory**

### Key Teaching Principle

**When God's people put His purposes first and faithfully obey His word, they can work with courage because God's presence—not human strength—is their foundation.**

The next book in the traditional sequence is **Zechariah**, which is considerably larger and more prophetic in its imagery, visions, Messianic prophecies, and connections to the New Testament.
